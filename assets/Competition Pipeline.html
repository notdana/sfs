Decide

Choose the right competition before involving students.

Competition List

Keep one list of all competitions the school may enter. This becomes the main place to compare opportunities.

  • Competition name and organizer
  • Type: technical, innovation, business, or mixed
  • Age/grade, team size, location, fee, deadlines
  • Required submission and competition date
  • Travel needs and expected budget
  • Status: watching, considering, approved, registered, completed
Competition List
Understand the Competition

Before saying yes, understand exactly what the competition expects.

  • Read the rules, scoring, judging criteria, deadlines, and required files.
  • Check what tools, components, software, or technologies are allowed.
  • Research past winners and strong past projects to understand the expected level.
  • Find the official contact person, email, phone, or support channel for questions.
  • Write down any questions and get clear answers before the team starts.
Competition Intake SheetRules Breakdown SheetCompetition Contact Log
Go / No-Go Check

Confirm the competition is realistic for the school.

  • Do we have eligible students?
  • Do we have enough time?
  • Can we afford the materials, fees, and travel?
  • Do we have the right mentor support and lab access?
  • Can we meet all submission rules?

A

Student Onboarding

Find students, test their skills, interview them, and build the right team.

A1. Student Outreach

Share the competition opportunity clearly with students before applications open.

  • What the competition is
  • What students may need to build or submit
  • Meeting times and expected commitment
  • Competition dates and deadlines
  • How students will be selected
Student Competition BriefStudent Interest Form
A2. Technical Test

Use a short practical test to understand each student's current technical level. The goal is to see strengths and training needs, not only to eliminate students.

ProgrammingLogic, variables, conditions, loops, functions, debugging, and basic code reading.
ElectronicsBasic circuits, components, sensors, power, wiring, and safe use of a multimeter.
Robotics & SystemsESP32, Arduino, Raspberry Pi, basic Linux, sensors, motors, control, and system thinking.
CADSketching, dimensions, simple 3D modelling, assemblies, and reading or creating basic technical drawings.
Recommended: score each area separately so the mentor can see who is strong in what. A student does not need to be strong in all four areas.

Use the interview to understand motivation, commitment, teamwork, problem solving, ownership, and communication.

Interview GuideInterview Rubric
A4. Select Students

Combine technical test results, interview results, availability, and competition needs. Do not select only the highest technical scores.

Create a balanced team with different strengths. Possible responsibilities include project lead, programming, electronics, robotics, CAD, research, documentation, and presentation.

Operations

Handle permissions, budgets, lab access, tools, and communication.

B1. Permissions

Track school approval, parent consent, travel permission, media permission, external testing, and any competition registration approvals.

Permission Checklist
B2. Budget & Ordering

Check what the lab already has before ordering. Track the item, quantity, cost, supplier, approval, order date, expected arrival, and who requested it.

Bill of MaterialsBudget TrackerOrder Tracker
B3. Lab Usage & Student Scheduling

Lab use includes both the physical lab rules and the process for bringing students to the lab at the right time.

  • Choose the session date, time, students, mentor, and purpose.
  • Check the students are available and not missing an important school requirement.
  • Inform the relevant people before the session: Principal, HMs, class teachers, parents, transport or security staff when needed.
  • Keep a record of who approved the session and who was informed.
  • On arrival: sign in, collect materials, review the session goal, and follow safety rules.
  • Before leaving: update documentation, return tools, store the project, clean the space, and sign out.
Lab Session RequestLab Communication ChecklistLab SOPLab Exit Checklist

Track reusable tools, consumables, controlled tools, damaged items, and items that need replacement. Students should know what can be taken, what must stay in the lab, and what must be returned.

C

Plan & Build

Move from rules to a reliable project, with clear teaching at each stage.

Short timeline: use the same stages, but make each stage shorter. Do not skip rules, design, testing, or documentation completely.
C1. Understand the Rules

Students learn how to read rules, scoring, restrictions, required files, and judging criteria. They should also review past winners and use official competition contacts to clear up unclear points.

Rules Breakdown SheetPast Winners Review
C2. Understand the Challenge

Define the problem, users, requirements, limits, success criteria, research needs, and existing solutions.

Challenge DefinitionRequirements Sheet
C3. System Architecture & Modularization

Break the full project into smaller parts so the team can build, test, replace, or change one part without rebuilding everything.

  • Identify inputs, processing, outputs, power, and communication.
  • Split the project into clear modules or subsystems.
  • Show how modules connect using block diagrams.
  • Design interfaces so a module can be removed or replaced easily.
  • Assign a student or sub-team to each module where useful.
System Architecture GuideBlock Diagram GuideModularization Guide
C4. Engineering Design

Electronics

Wiring diagrams, circuit diagrams, pinouts, power, voltage/current, components, and connectors.

CAD & Mechanical

Sketches, dimensions, 3D models, assemblies, exploded views, materials, and how parts will be made.

Software

Flowcharts, pseudocode, modules, functions, states, inputs/outputs, and data flow.

What to Teach

Teach only the skills the students need for the next project step, then apply them immediately to the project.

Wiring Diagram GuideCircuit Diagram GuideCAD Drawing GuideSoftware Architecture Guide
C5. Build Plan

Decide the build order, student responsibilities, materials, tools, software, dependencies, time, and risks. Build and test modules separately before joining them together.

Build & Integration Plan
C6. Build, Test & Improve

Build safely, test every important function, record results, and improve based on evidence. Keep failed attempts when they show why the design changed.

Testing PlanTesting LogIteration Record
C7. Student Workshops

Run workshops only when a skill gap is blocking the project. Possible workshops include programming, electronics, ESP32/Arduino/Raspberry Pi/Linux, robotics, CAD, manufacturing, testing, technical writing, pitching, or video.

Skills MatrixWorkshop Planner
C8. Competition Readiness

Prepare the team, project, equipment, and travel so competition day does not fail because of a small missing item.

  • Run full practice runs under competition-like timing.
  • Practice presentations, judging questions, setup, and reset.
  • Prepare replacement parts, spare electronics, batteries, cables, fasteners, and other likely failure items.
  • Prepare the tools needed for repairs at the competition.
  • Make a packing list and assign responsibility for each item.
  • Confirm transport: how students, mentors, projects, tools, and materials will get to the venue.
  • Confirm arrival time, meeting point, permissions, tickets, IDs, registration papers, and emergency contacts.
  • Verify every required digital submission before leaving.

Documentation

Capture evidence while the team works, not only at the end.

D1. Student Documentation

After each work session, students record what they planned, what they did, evidence, problems, decisions, and next steps.

Track attendance, progress, blockers, risks, purchases, skills that need training, deadlines, and mentor support given.

Mentor Tracker
D3. Photos & Videos

Capture early ideas, designs, CAD, wiring, building, testing, failures, improvements, user testing, and the final project. Take short useful clips instead of large amounts of random footage.
