Before you start: open your notebook

This guide doesn't hold your answers. Your engineering notebook does. Get it, turn to the first blank page, and write today's date at the top. Everything you write today becomes Entry 01.

Work through the four steps in order. For each prompt, copy enough of the question that it still makes sense when you read it back in a year or two, then write your answer beneath it. These questions aren't about what you already know. They're about how you think.

p. 03 Step 03

Finding Your Drive

What to do This is the most important section. Motivation that comes from someone else fades fast. Motivation that comes from inside you, from what you care about, what frustrates you, what you dream about, is what carries you through hard days. Let's find yours.
Tip You don't need to want to become an engineer or scientist to get enormous value here. A future lawyer who understands AI argues better cases. A future chef who understands design builds a better brand. A future athlete who understands entrepreneurship owns their career. These subjects belong to every ambitious person, including you.

  1. What do you want to do with your life, or what directions are you genuinely considering? Be real. "I don't know yet" is a completely valid answer.

    Write a full response in your notebook

  2. Whatever path you're imagining: which of the six subjects (robotics, programming, electronics, AI, design, or entrepreneurship) could give you an edge in that world? How specifically?

    Write a full response in your notebook

  3. What is a problem in the world that you find genuinely unfair, broken, or worth fixing? It can be big or small.

    Write a full response in your notebook

  4. Which of these six subjects could be a tool for tackling that problem, even if you're not sure how yet?

    Write a full response in your notebook

  5. Think of something you've built, led, created, or figured out that you're actually proud of. What was it? What drove you to finish it?

    Write a full response in your notebook

  6. Complete this honestly:

    "I want to understand because in the world I want to live in, or help build, it matters because ."

p. 04 Step 04

A Letter to Your Future Self

What to do You're going to write a letter to yourself, and a real platform called FutureMe will deliver it to your inbox in 1 to 3 years. Read by nobody else. Written only for you. This is one of the most honest things you can do on day one. Draft it in your notebook first so a copy stays with Entry 01, then transfer it.
  1. Go to www.emailtofuture.com on your phone or laptop.
  2. Set your delivery date 2 to 3 years from today.
  3. Use the prompts below to draft your letter, then transfer it to the site.
  4. Keep a copy somewhere safe. Then let it go.
Tip The most valuable letters aren't the polished ones, they're the honest ones. Write the version you wouldn't show anyone else. Future you deserves that.

  1. Who are you right now, honestly? What's taking up your head and your time this year?

    Draft in your notebook, then transfer it

  2. What are you hoping is true about your life by the time this letter lands? What are you afraid might be?

    Draft in your notebook, then transfer it

  3. What's one thing you don't want future-you to forget or give up on?

    Draft in your notebook, then transfer it

  4. What's one question you want future-you to sit with when the letter arrives?

    Draft in your notebook, then transfer it
