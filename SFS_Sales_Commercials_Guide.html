Training & Certifications

Every teacher is trained and certified before standing in front of students, and can grow through levels and specializations.

1
Level 1 : SFS Pilot Curriculum Certification
Delivers the SFS curriculum as designed, with the technical skills required to run the classes. By the end, the teacher can deliver the curriculum faithfully lesson by lesson, run the classroom using the SFS system of rules and group roles, administer and record assessments, and keep their class documentation and operations up to date.
2
Level 2 : SFS Full Curriculum Certification
Everything in Level 1, plus real technical capability to support students beyond the content. By the end, the teacher can build and debug circuits with sensors, motors, and microcontrollers, write and troubleshoot control code, assemble and program a responsive robot, use CAD including simulations, design and produce PCBs, train and use models on real data, and solve the common hardware and software problems students hit. This is Level 2, the minimum level required to move into a specialization.
3
Level 3 : Technical Master
Everything in Level 2, plus more advanced technical capability. This is Level 3. By the end, the teacher can implement control systems such as PID, work with ROS, handle communication modules and system integration, apply computer vision and edge AI on dedicated hardware, combine multiple technologies into one working system, and support open-ended projects with no predetermined solution.
Specializations

A teacher must hold Level 2 to move into any specialization. The exception is the Makerspace Lead role, which does not require Level 2 teacher certification.

Certified Makerspace Specialist
By the end, the teacher can safely operate and supervise the fabrication tools and machines, guide students in turning project ideas into real built objects, and maintain the Makerspace day to day.
Certified Innovation Mentor
By the end, the teacher can identify and grow standout students through the Innovation Hub, run innovation projects, research, and exhibitions, and guide students toward real-world innovation, industry exposure, and incubation.
Certified Competitions Coach
By the end, the teacher can mentor students through the engineering design process, coach the common competitions (Line Following, Maze, Missions, Robo Sports, Drones, Robo Fights), teach their technical foundations (CAD, wiring, modularization, PID, ROS), run the competition pipeline, and coach teams to compete beyond their own school.
Modern Technology Lab Curriculum

The SFS Learning Framework: what students learn and do, how they progress, and how learning is measured.

The SFS Learning Framework
How we teach: concept led
We teach the concept, not the tool. Students learn the underlying idea first, how a microcontroller works, the difference between analog and digital, and the tool is used only at the end to actualize that concept. The hardware serves the understanding rather than replacing it. This is what makes the learning transferable to any technology a student meets later, and it is why students are never locked into one product.

What Students Create
The Milestone Project

Alongside the lessons, every student runs their own project: a real problem they choose and solve. It moves through a four stage design cycle, one milestone at a time, where the teacher checks progress and keeps the work real.

M1
Define
Understand the problem, research the context, and write a clear problem statement and design brief.
M2
Design
Develop possible solutions, evaluate them, and choose one to build, with the reasoning documented.
M3
Build
Build a working prototype using the skills developing in parallel through the lessons.
M4
Test
Test against the original problem, refine, and finalize for the year-end exhibition.
How students are assessed: the 100 point structure

What makes us different, and how to say it
01
A connected system, not a bundle
Everything (curriculum, kits, tools, platform, training) is built to work together, not bought as separate parts from different vendors.Say it like this“Most schools end up with a robotics kit from one company, a curriculum from another, and training from a third. We give you one system where all of it actually works together.”
02
Start safe, grow with us
A school can start small, one lab, one pilot year, and grow when it is ready: to reach all students, to add the Makerspace, or to enter competitions.Say it like this“You don't have to bet big on day one. Start with one lab and a single pilot year, and we grow with you when you're ready.”
03
Concept and creation, together
Students learn the theory and build their own real projects at the same time, each reinforcing the other.Say it like this“Your students won't just follow steps to build a robot, they'll understand the ideas and create their own.”
04
We measure learning academically
Milestone projects measure what students can do; exams measure what they understand.Say it like this“At the end, you won't just have a robot on a shelf, you'll have proof of what each student actually learned.”
05
Everything is visible
Students, teachers, and leadership all see progress, strengths, and gaps on one platform.Say it like this“You'll be able to see exactly how the program is doing, student by student, on one dashboard.”

The Implementation

The school's journey once it signs and pays, from kickoff to a program running on its own.

1
Onboarding & kickoff
Contract signed and paid. Confirm the package, size, setup model, and timeline. Two people anchor the relationship: one school point person and one assigned SFS trainer.
School point person · SFS trainer
2
Procurement & build
The Procurement Manager sources the hardware, kits, and devices. The Engineer builds the lab (Model 2) or configures it after the school builds to spec (Model 1).
Procurement Manager · Engineer
3
Teacher training, part 1
Before the first lesson, the trainer delivers the first training and runs the first assessment.
SFS trainer
4
Communication
Before launch, the lab is communicated to the school, parents, and students, building awareness and excitement.
School · SFS
5
Launch
The lab opens, often with an inauguration. Curriculum, platform, and documentation go live, and the first lessons run.
School · SFS trainer
6
Teacher training, part 2
Mid-year, the trainer delivers the second training and second assessment. The teacher earns certification only after passing both assessments.
SFS trainer
7
Ongoing support & point-of-contact meetings
Across the year: support, competitions, the exhibition, and assessments. The assigned trainer holds a structured point-of-contact meeting with the school four times a year.
SFS trainer
8
Review & grow
At the end of the term, SFS reviews outcomes with the school, the proof of learning, which becomes the bridge to Grow and Own.
School · SFS
